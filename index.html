Overview

Agriculture is not one world. It is millions of them.

Same crop, two states. In Washington an apple orchard is a vertical fruiting wall: dwarf trees on a tall-spindle trellis, planted about three feet apart, fruit hanging in a flat plane a robot can approach from one side. In California's Central Valley around Lodi the same crop is often trained to a V-trellis: multi-leader trees angled on wires, wider between-row spacing, fruit on both faces of the V under harder heat and sunburn pressure, with reflective mulch and overhead cooling. Different approach angles, different occlusion, different glare. A policy trained on one breaks on the other, and that is one crop, two states. We build simulation that captures this variation faithfully, so a robot trained in it has, in effect, already seen your field.

Our Thesis

Agriculture is not one environment. It is a distribution of them.

A robot should not need to see your farm before it works on your farm.

Platform

Simulation infrastructure built for agriculture.

General-purpose simulators treat a field as a scene. We treat it as a living system with regional, seasonal and cultivar-level variation, and give you the tools to train and evaluate against all of it. The plant below is corn, not apple: the same stack models deformable physics across crops, so the point is variability: we can do this for any crop you run.

Physics of Living Plants

Deformable stems, branch compliance, fruit detachment forces, occlusion and contact modeled to match what a gripper feels in the field. Interaction realism is the part of the sim-to-real gap that matters most for manipulation, so it is where we spend our effort.

Evaluate, Then Train

Stress-test an existing policy across thousands of field variants to find where it breaks, then close those gaps with targeted domain randomization and RL. Built on Helios plant models and Isaac Sim. One command, any scale.

GRASP (2026): a policy trained on deformable plant physics in simulation, deployed zero-shot on a real arm.

Training

Train across the distribution

Thousands of fields, cultivars, seasons and contact scenarios run in parallel. The variation a robot would take a decade of field seasons to encounter is covered in a single training run.

Deployment

Deploy zero-shot

The same policy weights go straight onto the field robot. No per-farm data collection, no on-site fine-tuning. If the simulation is realistic and varied enough, the real field is just one more sample from the training distribution.
